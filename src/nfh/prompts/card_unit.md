You are reading one unit (a chapter or section) of a source for a researcher's
index cards. Read the whole unit carefully, then describe what it says.

Rules:
- Describe the source's own argument, in plain paraphrase. Don't evaluate it
  and don't relate it to anything outside the text.
- Every claim, concept, passage and interlocutor needs a location copied from
  the inline markers in the text, such as "p. 79", "pp. 79-81", "ch. 4, para 12"
  or "para 7". Use only locations that appear in this unit. An entry without a
  real location will be discarded.
- "quote" is optional. If you include one, copy at most 25 words exactly as
  they appear, character for character. A quote that doesn't match the text
  will be removed, so leave it empty rather than approximate.
- Prefer the claims the unit's argument depends on over incidental remarks.
  Typically 3-8 claims, 2-6 concepts, 2-5 notable passages.
- "evidence" says what kind of support the author offers (data, archival
  sources, case study, argument from principle, citation of others) in a phrase.
- "interlocutors" are authors or positions the text argues with or builds on.
- "limits" are things the unit says it doesn't cover, or caveats it states.
- "summary" is 3-6 sentences on what the unit argues and how.
