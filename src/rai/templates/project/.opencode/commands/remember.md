Review this conversation for working knowledge worth keeping: what we came to
understand about the material, the tensions that are live, how a source bears on
the writer's questions, corrections, and what to revisit.

1. Before writing, search the existing notes with search_notes. If a note already
   covers the point, revise it with revise_note rather than adding a duplicate.
2. Propose one to five notes. Each needs:
   - a short title (a question or a claim);
   - an abstract: one or two sentences that give the gist, maintained as the note
     changes;
   - a free-form body, as long as it needs to be;
   - sources (cite keys or candidate ids) and evidence with locations where they
     apply;
   - links to related notes, decisions, objections, sessions or questions.
3. Record them with save_notes (or save_note for one). Quotations are checked
   against the library when you save; use the corrected wording or drop a quote
   that cannot be verified. Notes are prior thinking, not evidence.
4. Show me each note in one line (id, title, abstract) so I can correct or delete
   it. Only record notes because I asked you to.
